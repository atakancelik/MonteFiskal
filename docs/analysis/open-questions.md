# Open questions

Everything the analysis could not settle from retrieved official sources. Nothing here was guessed into the other documents: the corresponding rules are marked UNVERIFIED there. Questions answered in a later round keep their number and row, marked **Answered**, so that references from other documents stay valid.

From the fourth round on, an answer that rests on desk research carries a **confidence** label: *high* = stated in an official text that was read; *medium* = follows from official texts by reasoning, or is stated officially only in general terms; *low* = analogy or practice only. "Not officially settled" means that no official Montenegrin text addressing the exact case was found; such answers are recommendations, not rules.

"Who can answer" uses these abbreviations: **TA** = Tax Administration of Montenegro (Poreska uprava), documentation page and fiscalization help desk; **efitest** = an experiment in the test environment with our own certificate; **Owner** = project owner decision; **Acct** = a Montenegrin accountant or tax adviser.

| Q-24 | Is a web order paid before shipment an "advance"? **Answer: formally yes; not officially settled for web shops.** Evidence: (1) the official FAQ: "subject to fiscalization are all invoices arising from the supply of products and services, **invoices arising from an advance payment**, and corrective invoices", and for advances it prescribes: invoice at receipt of the advance (items with their rates if the product is known, otherwise 21 %), at the real supply an invoice paid with `ADVANCE`, then a reduction of the advance invoice (D-13); (2) the VAT law requires an invoice for a payment received before the supply (D-06 Art. 31); (3) v5 defines exactly this three-document flow (BR-K9, D-09 example 1); (4) Serbia, with the same concept, treats a prepaid internet order as an advance receipt followed by a sale receipt (T-04, analogy, secondary source). Against: no Montenegrin text mentions web shops, the site search of the Tax Administration returns nothing on internet sales, and vendors advertise advance invoices only as a general feature. What is certain either way: the payment must be fiscalized **when it is received**, not at shipment (a card payment is a cash invoice issued at the moment of payment, BR-K1). Confidence: medium that the strict reading is "advance"; low on how inspectors treat a single invoice issued at payment for goods shipped a day later (tax-neutral, since VAT is due at payment in both cases). | Default mode of the plugin: one invoice per order, or three fiscal documents per order. | The taxpayer's accountant for the choice of mode; TA help desk for a written statement. | Both modes implemented and switchable per shop. **Default: one ordinary invoice fiscalized at the moment payment is confirmed** (never later); the advance flow is the documented strict mode, recommended for pre-orders, deposits and long delivery times, and the setup guide says so in plain words. |
| Q-25 | **Answered.** Every TCR must be registered, a web shop included; the same TCR may issue cash and non-cash invoices; registration is possible through the SEP or the web service once the taxpayer, business unit and operator exist on the SEP (D-05 Art. 3 item 7 and Art. 9, D-13, BR-K17, BR-R1). Remaining detail: whether one TCR per shop is expected when the shop runs on several servers. | Setup wizard flow. | TA. | The wizard registers one TCR per shop. |
| Q-26 | Software and maintainer codes for open-source software. **Answered from official texts** (BR-R7). (1) A company that uses the software and owns it may register it itself; copyright is not required (official FAQ, D-13): confidence high for the wording, medium for "owns" covering software taken under an open-source licence and operated on the company's own responsibility, which is our reading and is not stated anywhere. (2) The registrant must be a legal entity or entrepreneur registered in Montenegro or have a local partner (D-13): every merchant who can use MonteFiskal is a Montenegrin taxpayer, so each merchant registers itself as producer and as maintainer of its installation on the SEP (ROF first, then RSPOS; D-14 §3.2, §3.3); the mark is assigned automatically (D-12 Art. 15), no approval or certification step exists. (3) Every upgrade that changes functionality, and per the access instructions every new version, must be registered and gets a separate mark that is built in and sent on invoices; the taxpayer registers that version on its TCRs (D-13, D-14 §3.2): confidence high. Consequences: the project itself needs no registration as long as it does not act as producer or maintainer for anyone; the software code is a per-installation setting and must be changeable without reinstalling; each release states whether fiscal behaviour changed, and if so tells the merchant to register a new version on the SEP and enter the new code. Not officially settled: whether a release without functional change needs a new version entry (instructions say "new versions", FAQ says "change of functionality"). | The adoption path the project chose (no registered maintainer behind the project). | TA help desk for the open-source reading. | **Applied:** self-registration guide for the merchant; `SoftCode` and `MaintainerCode` are settings; release notes carry "fiscal behaviour changed: yes/no"; when yes, the plugin reminds the administrator to register the new version. |
| Q-27 | Is the specification's sample RSA private key (printed in the official technical specification, v02 and v5, for the IKOF example) acceptable to derive a public key from for a test vector? | The proposed spec vector uses only the public half. | Owner. | Use the public key only; do not commit the private key. |
| Q-28 | **Answered in the main:** a cash invoice must be printed on paper and handed to the buyer; a non-cash invoice may be electronic with the buyer's consent (BR-P4, v5). The 2021 FAQ says paper for every delivery; v5 is newer for non-cash invoices (D-13). Since consumer card and cash-on-delivery orders are cash invoices, a web shop puts a paper invoice in the parcel. Not officially settled: digital goods and services with no physical delivery; whether e-mail plus the buyer's own printing is tolerated. Confidence: high for the rule, none for the digital-goods case. | Whether the plugin's e-mail output is sufficient. | Acct; TA. | Provide both: e-mail block and a printable invoice with QR code of at least 21 × 21 mm; the setup guide tells shops with cash invoices to put the printed invoice in the parcel. |
| Q-29 | `CARD` or `BUSINESSCARD` for an online card payment? **Not officially settled; recommendation with low to medium confidence.** What the official texts say: `CARD` is a bank card issued to a natural person and belongs to a cash invoice; `BUSINESSCARD` is a bank card issued to a taxpayer and belongs to a non-cash invoice (D-07 Table 90, BR-K3); the law counts payment by card as cash payment and defines non-cash payment as a transfer between taxpayers' accounts and "every other payment that is not cash" (D-12 Art. 3). Nothing in the FAQ, the instructions, the opinion bulletin or the site search says how a seller should tell the two apart, and no vendor documentation found explains it. Reasoning: the distinction follows the **card holder**, not the buyer named on the invoice; a payment gateway reports at most the card brand and sometimes a "commercial card" flag; the seller therefore normally cannot know. Treating an unknown card as `CARD` errs on the stricter side (cash invoice: fiscalized at the moment of payment, deposit duty, paper invoice), which cannot be reproached as hiding cash turnover. | Chooses between a cash and a non-cash invoice, and with it the deposit duty and the paper requirement. | TA help desk; the shop's bank (whether its gateway reports commercial cards). | `CARD` for every card payment. `BUSINESSCARD` only when the administrator enabled it **and** the buyer is a company with a tax number **and**, where the gateway provides it, the card is flagged as commercial. |
